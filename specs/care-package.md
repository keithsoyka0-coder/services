## 1. The principle

Never leave the client in the dark. At handoff and completion, the client holds everything they would need. If a gap appears later, it is newly discovered or an addition — never something that should have been there in the first place.

## 2. The baseline package (must-haves — standard in every build)

Everything below ships with the base price. None of it is upsold.

- **Comprehensive `package.json` / scripts** — multi-function, documented, agnostic. The client can build, test, and deploy without asking us how.
- **No lock-in, ever.** Dockerized builds that deploy anywhere — Vercel, Netlify, AWS, their own infra. Whether we maintain it (extra, scoped) or they take the repo elsewhere, it is plug-and-play. The client never feels stuck.
- **Documentation suite** — README, quickstart, CurrentState, manifest, index, handoff document. The repo hygiene Keith holds for his own work, delivered as standard.
- **Workflows + skills** customized to their stack, platform, and services — not generic templates.
- **API flow document** — whenever the build touches APIs.
- **AI flow document** — whenever the build integrates AI.
- **Two AI pieces, not one** — the piece their system needs, plus the piece for *maintaining* that system (the collaborator sidekick). A signature.
- **Test suite, run green before handoff.** Nothing ships untested — least of all at these prices. The checklist comes back green or it doesn't ship.
- **Troubleshooting document** — the gaps we anticipated, answered in advance.
- **Provenance envelope on every file.** A signature.
- **Page contracts.** A signature.
- **License-key handoff** — full function on final payment; degraded mode without it. Non-payment doesn't get a free build.

## 3. Good-to-have (scoped add-ons, priced separately)

- Ongoing maintenance by us (extra, scoped — see §2, no lock-in).
- Workflows/skills beyond the baseline set.
- **Codebase / website audit + refresh** — not every venture is starting from zero; auditing and refreshing what exists is its own offer.
- Logo / branding (per the offering spec).

## 4. Not sold

- DI/AI where it doesn't fit. (Standing non-predatory rule.)
- Anything the client can't use or maintain after we leave.

## 5. Due diligence (internal — before anything ships)

- We meet our own specifications first. The spec is the median; the check is stereoscopic.
- Checkboxes, thorough, and we anticipate what the customer hasn't thought of — for the simplest landing page as much as the full build.
- The build must work *for them* beyond the site itself: their workflow, their downstream needs, their ability to run it without us.

## 6. Care ethics (outreach + engagement)

- **Non-predatory, always.** We don't sell where it doesn't fit, and we don't pitch people who aren't in a place to buy. Before approaching anyone — including Pancake leads — we contextually observe whether they're in a position to be approached. Dangling an offer in front of someone who can't afford it causes unneeded stress and makes us no different from generated outreach. We don't subscribe to that.
- **Positioning test:** nothing we offer should be something they can get anywhere else. Every offer must read, on its own merits, as an incredibly wise use of funds.
- **Bounded scopes** — every tier states what it includes *and* what it doesn't. (The quick-win guardrail against over-offering.)

## 7. Why this exists

Within a seven-day Pancake trial or any other channel, the differentiators are what make the outreach worth sending — and the ethics are what make it worth receiving. Quality is the moat; care is the brand.
